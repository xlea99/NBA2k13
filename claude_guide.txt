Yep so this is our Spritopia project, which aims to build a full ecosystem around NBA 2k13. You job is going to be to help us build the new gui - as you can see we already failed to do this twice, once with qt and then once with just using like a website which is an odd choice since we will run this entirely locally. Before we go forward, lets discuss the Archetypes, rarities, and artifacts. This all began organically where we were making custom players in NBA 2K13 for fun, taking them to "Blacktop" mode 4v4s, and playing first-to-21 (win by 2) games with them and doing funny voices. This evolved over time to a full roster of 200+ players, but we hit an issue - since in the create a player (CAP) menu in the game, you get to set 35 different attributes for the player, which correlate to how good or bad they play the game in different ways. some examples: "Quickness:	Agility while moving around the court", or "Low-Post Defense:	Ability to defend the post". Since we were just setting these bby hand, and since we wanted all new characters to be competitive with the current characters, over time this introduced aritificial inflation. By player 200, any new player basically HAD to be all 99s to be competitive.

We realized this was an issue, and we also realized that setting a custom player's stats isn't actually fun at all. To solve this, we designed a new system that utilized procedural generation of player stats on TOP of a player face we design ourselves (most of the time). The question became "how do we reliably generate interesting players?" We pulled out our game design hats and took all 35 attributes and divided them into 4 buckets: Offensive (attributes that effect a player's ability to score and play on the offense), Defensive (attributes that effect a player's ability to protect the ball, get rebounds, and be a nuisance to the enemy) and Control (attributes that effect a player's ability to move the ball around the court, make plays, etc). There's also General, which is a small bin for attributes that don't neatly fall into those previous 3, but its just 4 small attributes and you'll primarily hear us talking about the "three categories" which refers to Offensive, Defensive, and Control attributes. Then, we came up with this system - if we have three buckets of skills, why don't we procedurally generate players such that every player is good at exactly one, meh at exactly one, and bad at exactly one? We called this the 1-2-3 system or, eventually, the Primary-Secondary-Tertiary system. every single player that gets generated in the game always has a Primary that they're good at, a Secondary that they're okay at, and a Tertiary that they're bad at.

When you put all that together, you can already see where this is going - that necessarily creates exactly 6 archetypes of players, being the six possible combinations of appliying Offensive-Defensive-Control to Primary-Secondary-Tertiary:


Archetype   | Primary     | Secondary   | Tertiary
-------------------------------------------
Slayer      | Offensive | Control       | Defensive
Vigilante   | Offensive | Defensive     | Control
Medic       | Defensive | Control       | Offensive
Guardian    | Defensive | Offensive     | Control
Engineer    | Control   | Defensive     | Offensive
Director    | Control   | Offensive     | Defensive

You can basically figure out what each is good, meh, and bad at, and the type of players they are. Now this was good, this works really really well in our testing - players are very interesting and you can genuinely feel all 6 archetypes. However, as with any raw input -> output proc gen, it does create a patterny feeling if its too clamped. That's where we came up with the Rarity and Artifact system. The idea is that when we generate a new player (ie, when their archetype/attributes etc are rolled), they have a rarity, similar to like a hearthstone card. Common, Rare, Epic, Legendary, and Godlike. Any time we open a player ABOVE a Common, they get exactly 1 single artifact. You'll see the way we gen that in players.py's pmod system which compiles a pmod off of the fucking gargantuan artifacts.py file, if you're curious. All you really need to know is this: artifacts take a normal player and modify them. Rare artifacts are an accent, Epics are a twist, Legendaries are an overhaul, Godlikes are a clean slate. this creates tons of interesting fun while we're playing! They're inherently a little unbalanced, that's what makes it fun. Anyhow, that's how the whole players system works.

That's a very very general overview of the baseline system we're designing.

FAQ:

Q: Where are stat ranges listed? A: You can actually see the literal stat ranges all in spritopia/players/archetypes.py. We just hard code them from an external tracking spreadsheet (dont judge), and they are NOT the case of "every single primary stat has exactly 75-90", we hand designed each and every range for each and every archetype, we just took the assumption of "this attribute is supposed to be a secondary, so lets balance it contextually around what this attribute is against the realities of this archetype" you get the gist. Anyways, all the attribute ranges are there, you can look if you want but its not exactly interesting its just numbers
Q: How impactful are artifacts? Do they change minor parts of the player, or overhaul them? A: They can do all of the above. Godlikes especially can completely swap player stats. If you wanna look, you can check out spritopia/players/artifacts.py but its a monster file. It could be good to familiarize yourself with HOW artificats are built and fed/compiled onto a player object
Q: What is the actual flow of creating a player? A: Here is our intended final creation flow: First, we type in the Firstname/Lastname of the player, we specify an archetype and faction (or leave them blank to randomly pick). Then, once we've queued up a couple players to make, they get proc gen'd into the game and saved onto the roster using RedMC (you can see the flow api in spritopia/interface/red_mc.py). We reload the roster in NBA 2K13, then find the newly generated players - they will have generic blank faces, BUT all their attributes etc will already be present. Then, we just customize the face (or not, if during creation we selected a fully random face cause yes that's a thing too) and we're done
Q: Other gamemodes? What do you mean? And what is "Premier"? A: So this is a massive can of worms. This entire Player system and broader stat tracking/2K suite thing has a ton of potential and we want to do future game modes later, one of which is Gauntlet and more importantly, eventually a mode called League. For now, though, we default to a "mode" just called Premier, which is basically what we call "the normal game flow of just opening NBA 2K13, playing whatever game we want at random using the GUI's picker (which can automatically load players onto the game, see code in spritopia/interface/tracker.py on how we do this) and play for fun, with the stats of that game then being ripped and stored forever." Premier is just the default game mode, gauntlet is an absolute trip that will come later, think of it as like the difference between game modes in Hearthstone. Premier is "Play Now", Gauntlet is like an ever-revolving story mode type thing where absolute insanity happens. League is our attempt at creating a genuine league with all our players, with 8 competing teams + CEOs, prospects, subs, a pseudo-economy, playoffs, championship etc. That's what comes next but, for now, that's now the focus.
Q: What all does the GUI actually need to do? A: The GUI needs to do a TON. We have basically a full backend that allows us to interface with 2K to do basically whatever we want in it. We no longer even use the game's built-in player picker menu, the GUI needs to implement its own and then add them. It needs to provide the following functionalities: Player Search/viewing (attributes, stats, game history etc), Game Modes (Premier, Gauntlet/League eventually), Stats Center (just a bunch of cool ways to look at all the stats we're ripping with tons and tons of ways to sort), a Picker (the gui element we can use to actually load up a game, so like a picker would have 8 slots, we migth use a config screen to say the pick is 1-2-2-2-1, it would allow team 1 to pick one, then team 2 to pick 2, etc. There might even be like fun contraints (medic only game) shit like that. Then, the players get loaded onto actual NBA 2K13, and then we start a game), Create a Player screen (where we queue new players to be built, and then we can "open them" like hearthstone packs to see their rarities with sound effects and glamor), save functionality to actually save info between the 2K .ROS file, our db file, etc.
